# Exploratory comparison on the Lewis & Frank stimuli

This run responds to the 22 November 2026 discussion. Human outcomes and the candidate pairs were already known. The choices below were written before the new learner outputs were calculated. The work is exploratory, separate from the CIFAR addendum.

## Data and learners
Use lexical-consensus commit 23e4082b0bd7a33889714d0d179fa5a12791fb6c. Use the native cached unit-normalized embeddings for the ten primary representations and report three additional archived sensitivity variants separately (two random-network seeds and MAE CLS). Use all 24 test pictures and all 12 domain-by-teaching combinations. Original stimuli/support definitions: XTMEM commit 220ed17ec2b85d30a4053711209252fdbc3cb166, experiments/exp1/js/exp1.js. Verify reconstructed centroid distances against all human-data tables. The human outcome is the selection fraction, 600 sessions per domain, condition and picture, 520 distinct people overall. Repeated adult sessions do not constitute new synthetic runs.

## One word, same visual evidence
Fit a fresh unmodified lexical learner to the one or three actual teaching images and an anonymous label. Score all 24 test images. Use Centroid, KCentroid (K=2), ExemplarKNN (k=3 weighted), and Random as a floor. Logistic regression and linear SVM require at least two classes. Use seed 42 throughout. Compare the score order for the gray car and turtle after broad vehicle teaching. Also record the rabbit/radish after broad animal teaching and the within-domain disagreements discussed in the conversation. Scores within 1e-6 are treated as tied.

For three supports add a declared exploratory, symmetric acceptance rule. Place each test candidate alongside the three supports. For each of the four images, fit to the other three and score the held-out image for the target word. Accept the candidate if at least one teaching image has a score at or below its score (tolerance 1e-6). This permits any number of the 24 pictures to be selected. This new decision rule measures compatibility with support dispersion; a broader support set can itself broaden acceptance. Use no human response or test-category label. Skip this rule with one support, where reciprocal similarities tie for the geometric learners. Random has no interpretable support-based boundary, so report its score only in the single-word test.

Additionally compute, for each deterministic lexical learner and encoder, the largest possible increase in the fraction of target-domain superordinate items selected (broad three minus narrow three) over every common threshold on its raw score. Each set has twelve candidates (four per domain); a single threshold applies to both conditions and all three domains. This is a diagnostic upper bound, not a chosen operating point or an estimate of a human rate.

## Three words, original decision rule
Fit all six original learners on all three domains' support images under three distinct anonymous labels, one condition at a time. Use their native predict and score methods. An item accepts the target word if predict returns its ID. This task exposes competing category examples and forces one label per picture; comparisons with adult multiselection are descriptive. No category names, test labels or human choices are passed in fitting. Report both the broad-vehicle pair and generalization across narrow and broad teaching. Random baseline seed 42, no inferential tests or independent replication claims.
